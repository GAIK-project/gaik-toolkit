Let’s put it this way: a true magnification of two and a half in the Galilean loupes, like the ones you have, is very suitable for a dentist. Two times is enough, or is usually better for a dental hygienist, because they often need a slightly wider field of view. And magnification works in such a way that the more you magnify, the smaller both the field of view and the depth of field become. So you don’t want too much magnification, because that would compromise the other features. And then with these Keplerian loupes, the actual magnifications we’re talking about are perhaps between three and five, with different models in that range. Those are the ones people work with. If it says seven times or eight times, you can take that figure with a bit of a grain of salt.
