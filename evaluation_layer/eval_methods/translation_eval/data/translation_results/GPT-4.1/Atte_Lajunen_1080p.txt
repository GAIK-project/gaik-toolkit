Then we could go through these radiation protection training requirements for clinicians and oral health care professionals; in this case, we won’t go into the medical doctor side. And let’s start with the general dentist—what is the requirement at the moment? For a general dentist, there should be twenty hours of continuing education in every five-year period, so basically, when you look back five years, there should be twenty hours fulfilled. Right, and it doesn’t matter how it’s distributed over those five years, so it can be one big package or, in any case... Yes, it can be one big package or more spread out.

And then dental hygienists, dental assistants, who often take the images on the dentist’s instructions or orders. For them, of course, it starts with having the basic education in order and, on the other hand, also device training, so that the so-called button technique for the specific device is in order, and in addition to this, they also have the same twenty hours. And then if we think about the medical doctors, for dental radiologists, the requirement is forty hours, so there is a bit more.

What about if the practice has a CBCT device separately? Does that bring any additional requirements for this radiation protection training? Legally, not anymore. Previously, it was required to have certain courses completed both for the device users and for the doctor responsible for the procedures, that is, the one responsible for those CBCT scans, their justification, and so on. But nowadays, there is no specific course required by law anymore, but the normal continuing education, radiation safety training, should in practice also include topics related to CBCT training, and it is again the responsibility of the operator to ensure that their staff has sufficient competence both in using the device and also in terms of clinical imaging needs, image interpretation, and other related topics.
