If the dentist said it would take an hour and a half, and it ends up taking three hours, can you just carry on?

Well, there is a set amount of time. That’s why the dentist usually books roughly twice as long for it. It’s almost more common to finish in time and have extra time before the next patient. The schedules aren’t really flexible in that way, because they try to do children’s procedures fairly early in the morning. In principle, you might think they could do them last thing in the evening, and then they could let them run on quite a bit.

Right. And after that kind of general anaesthetic, the child spends some time in recovery and then goes home. Do the parents then keep a close eye on the child for the rest of the day? Of course, with a small child that goes without saying, but if it’s a slightly older child, does someone still need to watch them for the rest of the day?

Yes, and adults also need someone at home with them for the rest of that day, and they mustn’t drive. They mustn’t drive themselves home. With a small child, the parents are asked to come and be with the child quite soon after the anaesthetic is over. Usually a small child carries on sleeping, and then it’s nice for them to wake up with their mother or father there waiting and comforting them. Children are rarely in pain, either. The procedures generally aren’t painful, and the dentist will usually have numbed the sore areas.

Well, yes, that’s part of it. Midazolam, or Dormicum as it used to be called, of course wears off faster and takes effect faster too, which is good. It also has a stronger effect on memory, so the patient won’t remember an unpleasant experience, particularly a child.
