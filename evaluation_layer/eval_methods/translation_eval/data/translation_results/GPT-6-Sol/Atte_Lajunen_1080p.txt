Next, we could go through the radiation protection training requirements for clinicians and oral healthcare professionals. We won’t go into the medical side in this case. Let’s start with general dentists: what is the current requirement?

General dentists should complete twenty hours of continuing education in every five-year period. So, in principle, if you look back five years, those twenty hours should have been completed.

Right, and it doesn’t matter how they’re spread over those five years? It could be one big package, or—

Yes, it could be one big package or spread out more.

And then there are dental hygienists and dental nurses, who often take the images on a dentist’s instructions or orders.

For them, of course, the starting point is that their basic training is in order, and also their training on the equipment—that they know how to operate the particular device. In addition to that, the same twenty-hour requirement applies to them too. And if we think about dental radiologists, the requirement is forty hours, so it’s a little higher.

What if a practice has a separate CBCT unit? Does that add any requirements to this radiation protection training?

Not as a matter of legislation anymore. Previously, certain courses had to be completed by both the equipment operators and the dentist responsible for the procedures—that is, the person responsible for the CBCT scans, their justification, and so on. But the legislation no longer requires any particular course. In practice, though, the regular continuing education in radiation safety must also cover matters relating to CBCT, and it’s the operator’s responsibility to ensure that their staff have sufficient competence both to use the equipment and in matters such as assessing the clinical need for imaging and interpreting the images.
