## Task
Combines a meeting audio recording, the agenda document, and the participant list to produce a structured, evidence-cited meeting record capturing decisions, actions, and unresolved issues for project-manager review.

In plain terms: instead of a project coordinator spending time listening back through a meeting recording to write up the minutes by hand, the system does that first pass automatically. It reads the agenda and the participant list alongside the recording, and hands the project manager a ready-to-review draft that already separates out what was decided, who owes what and by when, and what is still open.

## Business need
**Current process:** A project coordinator listens to the meeting recording, checks the agenda and participant list, and manually prepares the meeting record. A project manager then reviews and approves the record; project team members use the approved record.

**Pain points:**
- Manual preparation is time-consuming
- Action tracking is inconsistent between meetings
- Decisions are difficult to trace back to their source (recording or agenda)

## Proposed solution
An AI-supported pipeline that combines the meeting recording, agenda document, and participant list to draft a structured meeting record -- separating agenda topics, discussion summaries, decisions, assigned actions, unresolved issues, and conflicts -- with each decision/action/issue/conflict backed by a traceable citation, for the project manager to review and approve.

By transcribing the recording and reading the agenda in the same pass, the system removes the manual listen-and-write effort while producing a more consistent structure every time (the same section headings, the same action-item shape) instead of quality varying by coordinator. Because every decision, action, unresolved issue, and conflict is tagged with a citation back to a timestamp or page number, tracing a record entry to its source is no longer a matter of re-listening to the whole recording. Where the agenda and the actual discussion disagree (e.g. a proposed date that was later changed), the system flags it explicitly as a conflict rather than silently picking one side.

The central knowledge object is the **meeting record**: it starts as three disconnected artifacts (an audio recording, a proposal-only agenda, and a plain participant list) and is transformed into one evidence-linked structured document where every decision, action, unresolved issue, and conflict can be traced back to the exact moment it was said or the exact agenda page it came from.
